Exercises That Support Testosterone Health

Strength Training

Resistance training supports muscle strength, physical fitness, and overall health. Testosterone may rise temporarily after exercise, but this does not necessarily mean a lasting increase in resting testosterone levels.

Recommended Exercises

  • Squats
  • Deadlifts
  • Bench Press
  • Pull-ups

Foods and Nutrition

Eggs

Eggs provide protein and important nutrients that support overall health.

Oysters and Seafood

Oysters are rich in zinc, a mineral needed for normal testosterone production. Zinc supplementation mainly helps when a zinc deficiency is present.

Fatty Fish

Salmon and sardines provide omega-3 fatty acids and vitamin D, supporting general health. They are not proven testosterone boosters.

Nuts and Olive Oil

These foods provide healthy fats that help maintain a balanced diet.

Sleep, Cortisol, and Testosterone

Why Sleep Matters

Most adults need 7-9 hours of sleep each night. Insufficient sleep may reduce testosterone levels, especially when sleep loss is prolonged.

Understanding Cortisol

Cortisol is a hormone that helps the body respond to stress. It is essential for normal health. Chronic stress can affect sleep and hormone health, but lowering cortisol does not automatically increase testosterone.

Healthy Habits

  • Maintain a consistent sleep schedule.
  • Aim for 7-9 hours of quality sleep.
  • Exercise regularly without overtraining.
  • Practice relaxation and deep breathing.
  • Avoid caffeine close to bedtime.
  • Limit alcohol consumption.

How Long Does Improvement Take?

Testosterone levels can change throughout the day. Improvements related to sleep, weight management, and lifestyle changes may take weeks or months. Results vary, and there is no guaranteed timeline.

Supplements and Testosterone

1. Zinc

Zinc is essential for normal testosterone production. Correcting zinc deficiency may help restore testosterone levels. Extra zinc is not proven to boost testosterone in people with adequate zinc.

2. Vitamin D

Vitamin D supports bone and muscle health. Correcting a deficiency is beneficial for health, but studies have not consistently shown that supplementation increases testosterone.

3. Magnesium

Magnesium supports normal muscle and nerve function. Evidence that magnesium supplements increase testosterone in healthy adults is limited.

4. Ashwagandha

Some small studies suggest that ashwagandha may reduce perceived stress and modestly affect testosterone levels in certain populations. However, evidence remains limited. Rare liver injury and medication interactions have been reported.

5. Creatine

Creatine can improve performance during resistance training and support muscle gains. It does not consistently increase testosterone.

Important Safety Information

Supplements are not substitutes for adequate sleep, exercise, balanced nutrition, or medical treatment. High doses can cause harm. Consult a healthcare professional before starting supplements, especially if you take medications or have medical conditions.
